---
title: "What's not there yet"
description: 'Know what ChapterJS does not do yet, how to recognise it, and what to do meanwhile'
---

ChapterJS is young. This page lists what it does not have yet, so that you do not look for it, and what to do meanwhile when there is something.

<Note>
  What exists is in the [reference](/reference): slash commands, events,
  components (buttons, select menus, forms, embeds), tasks, the presence, voice,
  translated messages and the structures they give you.
</Note>

## Context menu commands

Commands used by right-clicking a user or a message. Not available: a file of `src/commands/` is always a slash command.

Meanwhile: a slash command with a `user` option, or a `string` option that takes the link of a message.

## Prefix commands

Commands written in a message, like `!ban` or `?help`. Not available: a file of `src/commands/` is always a slash command, registered with Discord.

Meanwhile: a `messageCreate` event that reads `message.content`. It needs the [Message Content Intent](/events/messages#message-content-intent), and the person gets none of what a slash command gives: no list of options, no typing help, no permission check before your function runs.

## Waiting for an answer

Asking a question with a message, then waiting for the next message, reaction or click of the same person. Not available: a function answers, then ends.

Meanwhile: a button or a menu of `src/components/` that carries what it needs in its `data`. Its file runs when the person clicks, however long after, even after a restart: see [Components](/components).

## Storing data

Somewhere to keep what your bot learns: scores, settings, warnings. Not available: nothing survives a restart except the `data` of a [component](/components), which lives in the message itself.

Meanwhile: a JSON file written with `node:fs`, or any database made for Node.

## Other events

The [events](/reference/events) your files can react to are the ones listed there: messages, reactions, polls, typing, members, servers, channels, threads, roles, voice, presences, bans, the audit log, emojis, invites and scheduled events. What auto-moderation does, stickers, soundboard sounds, pinned messages, webhooks, integrations, stage instances and the status of a voice channel are not there yet.

## Recording voice

Hearing what people say in a voice channel, to record or transcribe it. Not available: your bot [joins and plays](/voice), and is deafened by default.

## User-installed apps

A bot that a person adds to their own account, to use its commands in every server and in private messages. Not available: a bot is added to a server, and that is where its commands are.

## Premium and entitlements

[`premiumButton()`](/reference/layout#premiumbutton) opens the store of your bot. Knowing who has subscribed, and being told when someone does, is not available.

## Audit log, scheduled events, stickers and auto-moderation

What your bot reads of them is [plain data](/reference/data), with nothing to do on it: no action to create, edit or delete one.

## A bot spread over several machines

A large bot runs on [several processes of one machine](/run/start#large-bots) by itself. Running it on several machines at once is not available.

## Choosing what the bot remembers

How many members, users and messages the bot keeps in memory is decided by the files of your project and by the memory available: see [What the bot remembers](/memory). It is not a setting you can change.

## A config file

There is no `chapterjs.config.ts`: what your bot does comes from the files of `src/`, and what it needs to run from [`.env`](/setup/project-structure#env). That is also why a project has one token and one test server.

## Testing your bot

Running your functions in a test, without Discord. Not available: nothing of ChapterJS is made for a test runner.

Meanwhile: keep what can be tested in plain files (the text of an answer, a calculation), and test those alone. A file next to your code named `*.test.ts` or `*.spec.ts` is left to your test runner, never loaded by the bot. The declarations of commands, events, components and tasks stay thin.

## Sharing files between bots

Publishing your commands or events as a package, to use them in another project. Not available: a file is only loaded from the `src/` of the project.
